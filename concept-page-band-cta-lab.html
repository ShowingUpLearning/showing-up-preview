An Explore button on every band

Ben, on the homepage: "we need to reintroduce an explore button here to take you to Assess", and the same for Toolkits and Play. He was right, and it was worse than it looked: two of the six bands had no route to their section page at all.

Scheme A is now live on the homepage, with the arrow-link secondary - go and look at the real thing, this page is the reasoning behind it. Kept here so the options Ben did not see are still on the record, and because two questions are still open for him: whether Play gets a real game behind a second action, and whether the band title becomes a link. If he prefers scheme B or C, the labels are one edit each.

Decision 1 · the button copy, as a set

Three schemes. The point of seeing all six at once is that the set has to read as a set - one odd phrasing shows up immediately when they are stacked.

A · Explore the thing

Extends the phrasing already live on Define, Learn and Lead, so three of the six bands do not change at all. Names what is behind the click rather than the section.

Decision 2 · do the secondary actions stay?

They should. The secondary actions are the only things on this page that do something real - a live assessment, a real PDF, a playable game - and they are what makes the homepage more than a contents list. What changes is the hierarchy: the Explore pill becomes the filled primary, and the action sits beside it as the quieter of the two.

No fill, no border, no underline at rest. Rollover: the label turns coral and the arrow slides 3px forward, which is the cluster tiles' "Explore" behaviour and the same motion as the title link in decision 3, so the page has one idea of what a forward move looks like rather than three.

Why this one. The brand rules already say it: pills are a deliberate choice for primary calls to action and are not to be used for secondary navigation. A ghost pill is still a pill. Dropping the capsule gives the band one button, so there is no question which route is primary, and it buys back horizontal room on the two bands whose secondary label is long.

Worth telling Ben, since this one is already decided: on 28 July he asked for exactly this kind of link to be taken off the Manage band and replaced with a button. The case was different - it was the only control in that band, so a quiet link was the wrong weight for the one thing you could click. Here it sits beside a filled pill, which is the situation the treatment is for. Flagged rather than quietly reversed; if he feels strongly, the ghost pill above is the fallback and nothing else in the proposal changes.

Also handled: the arrow holds still for anyone who has asked their system to reduce motion, and the keyboard focus ring is ink rather than coral, since coral fails the 3:1 non-text contrast bar on cream.

Decision 3 · should the number and title be a link too?

Ben's suggestion. It gives a much bigger target than a pill, and it is the pattern most people already expect from a card. Two ways to take it.

Title links, chip stays decorative

Recommended. The headline carries the link and the number stays decoration. Hover moves the arrow and turns the title coral, so it reads as clickable without an underline. Two links to one page in a band is fine; three would be noise for anyone using a screen reader.

One route in, one obvious control. Nothing to learn, nothing to mis-hit on a phone, and the band keeps a single call to action. The safe answer if the pills land well on their own.

What we need back from Ben

1. Which copy scheme - A, B or C?

A is the recommendation: three of the six bands already read that way, so it is the smallest change that makes the set consistent.

2. Does Play get a real game to point at?

If there is a playable challenge we can link, "Try a team challenge" becomes a genuine second action. If not, the honest move is to drop it and let the Explore pill stand alone on that band.

3. Title as a link, yes or no?

Worth a yes - it is free reach on mobile, where the pill is a small target and the headline is the biggest thing on the band.
