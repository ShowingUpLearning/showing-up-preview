Define (ultra) · layout options

One shape, nine times

The Define page reads evenly all the way down, and this is why: every block on it is built the same way. A heading and a paragraph, then a row of equal boxes across the full width. Nine blocks, nine times the same shape. Nothing is ever allowed to be bigger than anything else, so nothing leads the eye, and the page has no loud moment and no quiet one.

In five of those nine, the shape is also arguing with the words. Below are those five, each with the version now live on the preview for comparison, then two or three other ways it could be laid out. Every word in the examples is the page's own, unchanged.

Nothing here has been applied. It is a set of options to pick from, and no decision has been made.

What makes it read flat

Nothing is broken and nothing is off-brand. The spacing, the type and the colour are all correct. The page simply has one idea about structure and uses it everywhere, until every part of the argument carries the same weight.

One width. Text always the same measure, rows always the full width. One alignment. Everything flush to the same left edge. One rhythm. Identical space above all nine sections, so none of them can feel more important. One container. The same bordered card everywhere. One entrance. The same fade-in on every element.

The answer is not more decoration. It is letting a few blocks take the shape of what they are saying, and letting the page change its width and its breathing so the things that matter get to be bigger.

Now - every band the same weight

Nine bands, identical measure, identical padding. The page has no crescendo and no rest.

Possible - measure and breathing vary

A narrow reading band, a standard band, and two moments allowed to break the container - the document spread and the lead film.

C
Split rail: heading left, levels right

The cheapest structural change on the page, and the one that generalises. The heading and its framing copy move into a 360px left rail; the content runs in the right column. It breaks the "heading block, then full-width row" shape that every section repeats, and it drops the prose measure to a comfortable 45 characters.

Costs: nothing in content. Worth applying to two or three sections only - used on all nine it becomes the new monotony.

Section two

How the framework enables great development

The words are a sequence over time, and they end in a turn: the framework works so well that you stop needing it. Four equal cards side by side say the opposite, that these are four separate facts of equal weight. The best line on the page sits in the fourth box at the same size as the other three, wrapped over six lines.

NOW
Four equal cards

01 to 04 are numbered, so the reader is told there is a sequence, then shown a row of siblings.

  1. 01

    When you leave a client meeting that hasn't gone especially well, the framework gives you a way to talk about it: our curiosity was developing in there, but it wasn't yet fluent.

  2. 02

    It's also why the levels are numbered. Our clients will say 'that was Curiosity level three', meaning you were pretty solid, or simply 'Critical Thinking five'.

  3. 03

    Everyone works through the framework, assesses where they are against each dimension, and builds their development plan from it.

  4. 04

    And here is the best part: before long, you stop needing the framework in front of you at all, because everyone is speaking the same language. It stops being a document and becomes something that unites people.

A
The dissolve

A vertical run of four steps where the container itself weakens: full border, then half, then a trace, then nothing at all. The last step has no box, is set at 27px, and the connecting rail stops before it. The layout performs the argument - the scaffolding fades and the language is left standing.

Costs: a strong idea used once. It earns its place here because the copy's ending is genuinely a reversal; repeat the trick elsewhere on the page and it becomes a mannerism.

If you only change three things

  • Symbols → A, the specimen sheet. The most gain for the least risk. It removes nine boxes, changes no words, takes up less room than what is there now, and stops the section arguing with itself.
  • Development → B, three then the payoff. The same words, weighted differently, and the one place on the page where a single line gets to be big.
  • The document → A, the wide spread. Gives the page one moment that runs wider than everything else. That is what fixes the flatness across the whole page rather than one block of it.

Those three break the pattern in three of the nine bands, which is enough to give the page a rhythm. The five levels and the films come next, and both carry a question for you rather than for us: whether the career stages fold into the levels diagram, and which film leads.

Two things to know before you comment. Nothing here is applied - the Define page on the preview is unchanged, so you can flip between the two. And every word in the examples is the page's own, so if a layout looks wrong because the words are wrong, say that instead.
